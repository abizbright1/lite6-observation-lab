The research idea in ordinary language

Imagine that the camera sees a block and reports its location. The robot needs to know where the block is relative to the tool at the time the movement will matter. Three different questions arise:

  1. What did the camera observe? This is a perception question: pixels, features and object detection.
  2. Where and when does that observation belong? This is a calibration, coordinate-frame and timing question.
  3. Is the remaining error small enough for the proposed movement? This is the task decision.

A camera can confidently recognize a block while its position information is wrong for the current robot pose. Conversely, an old observation of an unchanged scene can still be adequate. The research should not label every old image dangerous or every confident detection trustworthy.

An electrical-controls analogy is a PLC permissive. A sensor input can be ON, but a machine transition may still require the right mode, position and timing. Here the scientific question goes beyond writing another permissive: can we calculate the remaining positioning uncertainty accurately and efficiently enough to make useful decisions? Existing research already covers many ingredients. Your proposed contribution must be demonstrated against strong methods, not assumed because the system uses AI or a robot.

The initial physical task is deliberately limited: a stationary suction-compatible object on a known plane; a camera mounted on the robot tool; one observation height and orientation; planar alignment followed by bounded vertical descent. Moving targets in one supplied mathematical illustration teach uncertainty dependence; they do not expand the physical task automatically.

The logic you should understand before laboratory work

A measurement is not the true position

The real target position is X. A sensor-derived position is z. A model describes how z could differ from X. An estimate chooses one plausible value. An uncertainty set retains several plausible values. A validation reference is a separate measurement arrangement used to assess the estimate; it also has uncertainty.

Do not mix these roles. In a simulator, the programmer knows X exactly because it generated the example. A real monitor does not get to read X. Giving simulated truth to the monitor would make the experiment circular. In Foundation Lab, actual target position and true delay are stored under evaluator_only; the monitor receives only public inputs.

Units must survive every calculation

At a constant relative speed of 60 mm/s, 30 ms corresponds to:

30 ms = 0.030 s
60 mm/s × 0.030 s = 1.8 mm

This is a one-axis constant-speed displacement calculation, not a universal error bound. If acceleration, camera rotation, calibration or stop/settle error matters, the model needs additional terms. If neither camera nor target moves and the geometry is unchanged, age alone does not create this displacement.

Capture, receipt and action have different times

exposure -> camera processing/buffering -> host dequeue
         -> feature computation -> decision -> command -> physical outcome

In a synthetic common-clock example, exposure is 10.000 s, dequeue is 10.080 s and decision is 10.090 s. Exposure-to-decision age is 90 ms. Time since dequeue is only 10 ms. Both numbers can be correct, but they answer different questions.

Your inspected camera producer records host dequeue time in the freshness path. It does not preserve original exposure timing in that path, so its dashboard age cannot establish exposure-to-action latency. A future research logger needs the original message timestamp and exposure convention, source frame ID and separate host events. This finding is from a read-only source inspection, not a new hardware measurement. (Equipment audit).

Clock offset is the difference between clocks. Latency is the time taken through a process. Jitter is variation in timing. Age is elapsed time since the information was generated. A monotonic clock is useful for elapsed time on its own clock domain; it does not automatically synchronize the camera, robot and host. Never subtract raw device time from host time without a documented mapping. (Python time, DepthAI timestamps).

Pixels need geometry before they become robot coordinates

An image coordinate is in pixels. A robot target is typically in millimetres relative to a named frame. Intrinsics describe the camera projection; extrinsics describe relationships between frames. A simplified pinhole equation is u = fx × X/Z + cx, with X and Z expressed in the camera frame.

With fx = 800 pixels and a front-parallel point at Z = 400 mm, one pixel corresponds locally to approximately 0.5 mm in X. At Z = 800 mm it corresponds to approximately 1 mm. This simplified relationship does not account for distortion, tilt or arbitrary 3D geometry. The same pixel error can therefore imply different physical errors. Low reprojection error says the fitted model reproduces observations; it is not independent proof that the tool reaches the correct base-frame location. (OpenCV camera geometry).

Start with an understandable one-axis model

For a static target observed by a camera translating at constant velocity, using an old image together with the current camera position can give:

z = X + v × age + bias + noise
X = z - v × age - bias - noise

Here z is the apparent world position, v is signed camera velocity in mm/s, age is seconds, and bias/noise are millimetres in a simplified local metric model. The sign follows the stated camera-motion convention. A moving target viewed by a fixed camera is a different model; do not copy signs between them.

Suppose z = 102 mm, v = 60 mm/s, age is between 0.020 and 0.060 s, bias between -0.6 and +0.6 mm, and noise between -0.1 and +0.1 mm. Evaluating the allowed extremes gives:

lowest possible X = 102 - 60×0.060 - 0.6 - 0.1 = 97.7 mm
highest possible X = 102 - 60×0.020 + 0.6 + 0.1 = 101.5 mm

If the proposed tool position is 100 mm, every value in that interval is within ±3 mm, but not within ±2 mm. The largest discrepancy is 2.3 mm. This is the idea behind a task-error check: test all positions admitted by the model, not only the most convenient estimate. Its validity is conditional on correct assumptions. The teaching code uses ordinary floating-point arithmetic; it is not a certified numerical library or a physical safety mechanism.

Joint uncertainty means some combinations are impossible

In the separate delay–bias illustration, calibration produces bias = 100 × delay, with delay in seconds and bias in millimetres. A delay of 0.05 s must accompany bias 5 mm. A rectangle that independently allows any delay from 0 to 0.1 s and any bias from 0 to 10 mm also includes impossible combinations such as delay 0 and bias 10.

Retaining the relationship can give tighter predictions. This is an established idea; showing it once does not make it your PhD contribution. The research must compare against strong methods that already retain joint dependence, including a full joint solver. The Foundation Lab ambiguity lesson is a noiseless moving-reference illustration and is explicitly separate from the static-target laboratory scope.

More images do not always identify the cause

Identifiability asks whether different parameter values can produce the same observations. If all observations reveal only a combination of delay and spatial bias, repeating the same conditions may never separate them. Likewise, in a translation-only target-viewing model, target position and an extrinsic translation can appear through their sum. A stationary image can help with the timing contribution while leaving that spatial ambiguity.

An independent reference, a validated prior or genuinely informative motion may be needed. Some information cannot be recovered from the existing inputs. If every plausible explanation predicts an acceptable action, full parameter identification may be unnecessary. If plausible explanations disagree and the difference cannot be resolved, abstention is an appropriate outcome.

Assumptions are part of the result

An assumed error bound is not a detector confidence score. The largest error in ten training examples is not a guaranteed bound on the next observation. A 95 percent interval is not an all-cases guarantee. Bounds fitted from data need an explicitly stated statistical interpretation; temporal dependence and adaptive sampling complicate that interpretation.

If no model fits the observations, report inconsistency and withhold the proposed action. Do not silently widen bounds until a desired action passes. If a model still fits, it can nevertheless be wrong. Independent validation is needed to detect plausible-but-incorrect assumptions.

How this becomes an actual research project

First, understand and reproduce the small models. Then inspect whether the station has a meaningful unresolved error regime. Establish a reference that can resolve that error. Reproduce strong existing methods on the same histories and timing budget. Only after that attempt a specific new computational result and plan bounded physical evaluation.

The initial novelty question is not whether confidence checks are helpful. It is whether your particular coupled model permits a new accurate/efficient calculation that strong joint methods do not already provide. Task-directed identification, joint calibration, uncertainty sets and action gating already have precedents. Week 15 introduces the closest papers after you can understand what they claim.

There are two separate gates. Personal readiness means you can derive the examples, explain units and assumptions, reproduce comparisons and distinguish evidence from guesses. Research readiness means adviser/concentration fit, accessible equipment, independent reference, useful measured parameter regime and an unresolved contribution. Passing the course does not automatically pass the novelty gate.

If a genuinely fresh exposure after the camera settles already makes timing error negligible relative to tolerance, document it. Do not manufacture importance by selecting unrealistic delays. If all you can demonstrate is a useful implementation, that can remain an applied study while the dissertation question changes. Your earlier doctoral blueprint remains the detailed map of these scientific conditions.
