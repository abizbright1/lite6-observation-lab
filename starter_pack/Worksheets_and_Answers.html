1 Units

Calculate displacement for constant relative motion: (a) 60 mm/s and 30 ms; (b) 100 mm/s and 30 ms; (c) 120 mm/s and 25 ms. What happens if the milliseconds conversion is omitted?

Answer: (a) 1.8 mm; (b) 3 mm; (c) 3 mm. Convert delay to seconds by dividing by 1000. Omitting that conversion makes the numerical displacement 1000 times too large. A stationary scene/camera has no displacement from age alone under this model.

2 Timestamps

Assume all three timestamps are already expressed on one justified clock. Exposure: 10.000 s; host dequeue: 10.080 s; decision: 10.090 s. Find exposure-to-decision age, dequeue-to-decision time and exposure-to-dequeue time. What if exposure is raw device time while decision is host time?

Answer: 90 ms, 10 ms and 80 ms respectively. With different unmapped clock domains, those subtractions are not established elapsed times. Python monotonic time does not itself synchronize a camera clock. A newly assigned receipt time cannot prove a new exposure.

3 Coordinate frames

A planar camera-to-world transform is x_world = 100 - y_camera; y_world = 50 + x_camera. Find the world position of camera point (10,20) mm and recover it using the inverse. What rotation is represented?

Answer: (80,60) mm. The inverse gives x_camera = y_world - 50 = 10 and y_camera = 100 - x_world = 20. With standard right-handed planar axes, the rotation is +90 degrees followed by the translation (100,50). Naming a transform's direction is necessary; do not assume it can be used backward unchanged.

4 Pixel scale

In the simplified pinhole model u = fx X/Z + cx, let fx = 800 pixels. How many millimetres correspond locally to one horizontal pixel at Z = 400 mm and at Z = 800 mm? Why is this not a complete robot calibration?

Answer: Z/fx gives 0.5 mm/pixel and 1 mm/pixel. The calculation uses camera coordinates and fixed depth; it omits distortion, arbitrary plane orientation and the camera-to-tool/base relationship. It does not independently measure tool placement error.

5 A possible-target interval

Use z = X + v age + bias + noise. Let z=102 mm, v=60 mm/s, age in [0.020,0.060] s, bias in [-0.6,+0.6] mm and noise in [-0.1,+0.1] mm. Find the possible X interval. For a command at 100 mm, test tolerances ±3 mm and ±2 mm.

Answer: X lies in [97.7,101.5] mm. The largest absolute discrepancy from 100 is max(2.3,1.5)=2.3 mm. It passes ±3 and fails ±2. A point-only check at z=102 would pass ±2, illustrating how the decision changes when uncertainty is retained. This result depends on the model and bounds being correct.

6 Dependence

Calibration says bias = 100 mm/s × delay, with delay in [0,0.1] s. Give three feasible pairs and one pair admitted by independent bounds that violates the relationship.

Answer: (delay,bias)=(0 s,0 mm), (0.05 s,5 mm), (0.1 s,10 mm) are feasible. (0 s,10 mm) is inside the separate ranges but violates the relationship. A joint set can exclude it. This is established mathematical reasoning, not an original PhD result.

In the supplied separate moving-reference illustration, runtime velocity 100 mm/s makes all coupled contact positions 205 mm; the independent hull is [195,215] mm. Explain why a full joint solver can retain the same advantage.

Answer: It already preserves the delay/bias relationship. Beating the rectangle therefore does not show an advantage over all existing joint methods. The example holds a nominal command fixed and does not compare optimized action policies.

7 Identifiability

If an observation is y = target_position + bias, consider target/bias pairs (100,2) and (101,1), all in mm. Does a repeated reading of 102 mm distinguish them? What could help?

Answer: Both produce 102 mm, so repetition alone does not distinguish them. An independently known target position, separately measured bias or another informative measurement model could help. A stationary image is not a universal calibration solution. If the unresolved spatial error alone exceeds task tolerance, abstention may remain necessary.

8 Feasible sets

Intersect X in [0,5] mm, X in [2,6] mm and X in [3,4] mm. Then add X in [7,8] mm. What should a decision system do? Does a nonempty intersection prove that the assumptions are right?

Answer: The first intersection is [3,4] mm. Adding [7,8] makes it empty. Withhold the proposed action and diagnose model, data, bounds or associations; do not silently replace the model to force acceptance. A nonempty intersection only establishes internal consistency with the assumed model, not that the true world is included.

9 Optimization without installing a solver

Let 0 <= a <= 2 and 0 <= b <= 2-a. Find the minimum and maximum of f=a+2b. Compare the answer with independent bounds a,b in [0,2].

Answer: The joint feasible triangle has vertices (0,0),(2,0),(0,2). The linear objective takes values 0,2,4, so its range is [0,4]. The independent square also admits (2,2), giving a maximum 6. For a bounded polygon a linear objective reaches an extremum at a vertex. This does not mean a coarse grid finds exact extrema for arbitrary nonlinear problems.

10 Denominators

A point rule approves 12 including 4 invalid; an interval rule approves 6 including 1 invalid; an always-reject rule approves none.

Calculate approval fraction, invalid approvals per total opportunity, invalid approvals per approval, and valid opportunities rejected for each rule.

11 Fairness and truth leakage

One method can request five new images, but its comparator gets one. One monitor is passed true simulated delay while the other gets only declared metadata. Two thousand adjacent frames from one physical cycle are randomly split between training and test. Identify the problems.

Answer: Unequal observation budgets confound the algorithm comparison. Evaluator-only true delay leaks privileged information. Adjacent correlated frames cross the split and do not represent independent held-out cycles. Use common information/budgets, separate truth from inputs, and split complete episodes or sessions. Actual physical success exists only for executed actions; replay does not create the missing counterfactual outcomes.

12 Is the topic worth continuing?

The task uses a static block, the camera takes a genuinely new exposure after settling, measured remaining timing/geometry error is small compared with the task tolerance, and an existing full joint solver already supplies the required bound within the decision deadline. Should you add a larger AI model to claim novelty?

Answer: No. These observations challenge the proposed practical and computational gap. Document the result, review it with the adviser and narrow or change the research question. A different method name, another robot or an unnecessary network does not establish original knowledge. Receiving an old queued frame after stopping would not satisfy the genuinely-fresh-exposure condition, so verify that distinction first.

13 Plan the corrected time budget

You reserve a three-hour core every Monday through Friday and optionally add one hour on each of those days. Saturday and Sunday have no study. Calculate (a) the minimum/maximum weekly reserved hours; (b) the 16-week totals; (c) focused versus break hours if the core includes two 15-minute breaks daily. Where does Friday's adviser review belong?

Answer: (a) 5 × 3 = 15 hours and 5 × 4 = 20 hours per week. (b) 16 × 15 = 240 and 16 × 20 = 320 reserved hours. (c) Core focused work is 5 × 2.5 = 12.5 hours weekly, or 200 over 16 weeks; breaks total 2.5 hours weekly, or 40 over 16 weeks. All optional hours add 80 focused hours, giving 280 focused hours plus 40 break hours at the maximum. Friday's review fits the core's last 30 minutes; it is not an extra meeting. Weekends remain zero. A missed task moves to another weekday or extends the plan.
